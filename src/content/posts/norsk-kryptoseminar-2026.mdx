On 23 October I'm heading to Bergen for Norsk Kryptoseminar 2026, the annual gathering of the Norwegian crypto community, organised by Simula UiB. The seminar runs for a day at Storsalen at Læringsarenaen, and part of the programme is given over to short presentations from PhD students — I'll be giving one of them.

## The talk

My slot is 10 minutes, under the title *Privacy-Preserving Encryption Using Fully Homomorphic Encryption*.

The short version of what it covers: fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) lets you run computations directly on encrypted data and get back an encrypted result that only the data owner can decrypt. The party doing the work never sees the plaintext. That's a useful property whenever computation happens on infrastructure you don't control — cloud servers, third-party ML pipelines, shared platforms.

I started the PhD at NTNU in August 2026, so this is early-stage work. Ten minutes is about the right length for where I am: the problem I'm circling, why FHE is the tool I've picked up, and what I'm reading while I narrow down a specific research question. I'd rather present that honestly than oversell a result I don't have yet.

## Why it matters to me

This is the first time I'll present the PhD work outside NTNU. Mostly I want the room: people who have been working on cryptography in Norway for years, in one place, for a day. Getting my framing in front of them early — and hearing what they push back on — is worth more right now than a polished set of slides.
