## 01 Context

Transformer-based architectures dominate modern computer vision, but their self-attention layers scale quadratically with sequence length. Visual Mamba (ViM) applies the Mamba state-space model — originally built for long sequences in language modelling — to vision tasks instead, promising comparable performance with linear-time complexity. My MSc thesis at NTNU (2024–2026) investigated how well that promise holds up.

## 02 Approach

The work involved implementing and training ViM-based architectures in PyTorch, benchmarking them against transformer baselines on standard vision tasks, and digging into where the state-space formulation helps and where it falls short compared to attention. This built directly on coursework and TA experience in NTNU's Computer Science programme, alongside independent reading of the Mamba and ViM papers.

## 03 Result

The thesis was completed as part of my MSc in Computer Science (AI) at NTNU. It also became the starting point for what's next: I'm continuing this line of research as a PhD student at NTNU, starting August 2026.
